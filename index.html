01 — Story

From the Atlantic
coast of Morocco

I grew up in Safi, one of Morocco's oldest cities — a port town on the Atlantic coast shaped by maritime life and ceramic craft. For centuries, its artisans have transformed local earth into some of the most recognizable ceramics in the world, a tradition passed down through generations and still very much alive today. Growing up surrounded by that taught me something early: precision and beauty are not opposites. Making something rigorous and making something meaningful are, in the end, the same act.

From childhood I moved between mathematics and art — drawing, making music, finding patterns in things that seemed unrelated. That curiosity never left me, and it is what eventually drew me to data analytics: the belief that behind every dataset there is a story waiting to be made legible.

"I believe data, like art, tells a story — and the analyst's job is to make that story legible to the people who need it most."

After placing 87th out of 2,485 candidates in Morocco's national preparatory classes, I studied industrial management engineering at UM6P EMINES. Then came three years at OCP Group — the world's largest phosphate exporter — where I built production analytics systems that ran in the real world, under real constraints.

In 2025, I was selected as a Fulbright U.S. Study Grantee — one of just 7 Moroccans chosen that year — to pursue my MSBA (STEM) at Simon Business School, University of Rochester. It felt like the natural next step: bringing everything I had built in Morocco into conversation with the frontier of the field.
